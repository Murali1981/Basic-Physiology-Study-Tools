DNA Synthesis & Cell Division

Brief summary notes

DNA Replication

  • DNA helicases unwind the double helix, forming a replication fork
  • Free nucleotides pair with exposed bases (A–T, G–C)
  • DNA polymerases join the new nucleotides into complementary strands
  • Replication is semiconservative: each new DNA molecule has one old and one new strand

The Cell Cycle

  • Interphase
    • G₁ – growth & normal function; genes actively transcribed
    • S – DNA synthesis (replication)
    • G₂ – preparation for mitosis; chromosomes condense
  • M phase – mitosis (prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase) + cytokinesis
  • Some cells divide frequently (skin, gut lining); others rarely or never (adult muscle)

Cyclins, Oncogenes & p53

  • Cyclins activate cyclin-dependent kinases to drive the cell cycle (e.g., cyclin D in G₁)
  • Overactive cyclin genes → oncogenes → uncontrolled division (cancer)
  • p53 = key tumor-suppressor gene; activated by DNA damage → arrests cycle or triggers apoptosis
  • Mutated p53 found in >50% of cancers

Cell Death

  • Necrosis – pathological; cells swell and rupture
  • Apoptosis – programmed cell death; cells shrink, membranes bleb, nuclei condense
  • Pathways: extrinsic (death ligands/receptors) or intrinsic (mitochondrial cytochrome c release)
  • Both activate caspases (“executioner” enzymes) → DNA fragmentation
  • Essential for development, tissue turnover, and eliminating damaged/cancerous cells

Mitosis

  • Produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells
  • Stages: prophase (chromosomes visible) → metaphase (line up at equator) → anaphase (chromatids separate) → telophase + cytokinesis
  • Centrosome (with centrioles) organizes the spindle fibers that pull chromosomes apart

Telomeres

  • Protective DNA-protein caps at chromosome ends
  • Shorten with each replication (~50–100 bp lost per division)
  • Critical shortening → senescence or cell death
  • Telomerase rebuilds telomeres in germ cells, stem cells, and most cancer cells → unlimited division

Hypertrophy vs Hyperplasia

  • Hyperplasia – increase in cell number (most growth; e.g., callus)
  • Hypertrophy – increase in cell size (e.g., muscle after weight training; heart in hypertension)
  • Atrophy = decrease in size from disuse or disease

Meiosis

  • Occurs only in gonads to produce gametes (sperm & ova)
  • Two divisions: reduces chromosome number from 46 → 23 (haploid)
  • Meiosis I – homologous chromosomes separate (reduction division)
  • Meiosis II – sister chromatids separate (similar to mitosis)
  • Produces four haploid cells (in males); one mature ovum + three polar bodies (in females)
  • Genetic diversity from random orientation of homologues + crossing-over

Epigenetic Inheritance

  • Heritable changes in gene activity without altering DNA sequence
  • Mechanisms: DNA methylation of cytosines; histone modifications (acetylation/methylation)
  • Can silence genes permanently (in gametes or early embryo)
  • Contributes to cancer, fragile X syndrome, and other diseases
