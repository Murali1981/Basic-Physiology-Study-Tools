Cell Nucleus & Gene Expression

Brief summary notes

Nucleus Overview

  • Most cells have one nucleus (exceptions: skeletal muscle = many; mature RBCs = none)
  • Enclosed by a double nuclear envelope; outer membrane continuous with ER
  • Nuclear pores (via nuclear pore complexes) allow selective, energy-dependent transport of proteins and RNA
  • Nucleoli (dark, non-membrane regions) contain DNA that codes for ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
  • Gene expression = transcription (DNA → RNA) + translation (RNA → protein)

Genome & Proteome

  • Genome ≈ 25,000 genes (Human Genome Project); DNA is 99.9% identical among people
  • Proteome = all proteins produced; >100,000 different proteins
  • One gene can yield multiple proteins via alternative splicing, different polypeptide combinations, and post-translational modifications (methylation, phosphorylation, cleavage, etc.)

Chromatin

  • DNA + histone proteins = chromatin (forms chromosomes)
  • DNA winds around histone cores → nucleosomes
  • Euchromatin – extended, active in transcription
  • Heterochromatin – highly condensed, permanently inactivated genes
  • Histone acetylation opens chromatin → allows transcription; deacetylation closes it
  • Only ~300 genes active in any given differentiated cell; stem cells retain broader potential

RNA Synthesis (Transcription)

  • RNA polymerase opens DNA at promoter regions; transcription factors (e.g., hormones) activate specific genes
  • Only one DNA strand is transcribed; base pairing: G–C, A–U
  • Produces four main types of RNA:
    • pre-mRNA → processed to mRNA
    • mRNA – codes for proteins
    • tRNA – decodes mRNA during translation
    • rRNA – structural/catalytic part of ribosomes

mRNA Processing & Alternative Splicing

  • Genes contain coding regions (exons) and non-coding regions (introns)
  • pre-mRNA is cut and spliced by snRNPs (spliceosomes) to remove introns and join exons → mature mRNA
  • Alternative splicing allows one gene to produce multiple mRNAs and proteins (average ~3 proteins per gene)
  • Most human genes undergo alternative splicing; greatly expands the proteome

RNA Interference (RNAi)

  • Regulatory process that silences specific mRNAs (Nobel Prize 2006)
  • Mediated by short RNAs: siRNA and miRNA
  • These enter the RISC complex and either destroy the target mRNA or block its translation
  • One miRNA can silence many different mRNAs
  • Potential medical uses: suppress disease-causing genes or viral genes
