Plasma Membrane & Associated Structures

Brief summary notes

Overview

The cell is the basic unit of structure and function. It has three main parts:

  • Plasma membrane – selectively permeable boundary; enables communication and movement
  • Cytoplasm & organelles – fluid (cytosol) + specialized structures
  • Nucleus – contains DNA; directs cell activities; has nucleoli (ribosome production)

Structure of the Plasma Membrane

  • Mainly phospholipids (bilayer) + proteins + some carbohydrates
  • Hydrophobic core restricts water-soluble molecules; proteins enable selective transport
  • Fluid-mosaic model: lipids and proteins move laterally; proteins form a changing mosaic (often patchy, not uniform)
  • Peripheral proteins (partially embedded) vs integral proteins (span membrane)
  • Functions of membrane proteins: support, transport, enzymes, receptors, antigens
  • Carbohydrates (glycoproteins/glycolipids) on outer surface → blood type antigens, cell interactions, surface charge

Bulk Transport

  • Phagocytosis (“cell eating”): neutrophils & macrophages extend pseudopods to engulf particles/bacteria → food vacuole → fuses with lysosome. Important for immunity and clearing apoptotic cells.
  • Endocytosis: membrane invaginates
    • Pinocytosis – nonspecific uptake of fluid & large molecules
    • Receptor-mediated endocytosis – specific molecules bind receptors → vesicle forms (e.g., cholesterol uptake; also used by some viruses)
  • Exocytosis: secretory vesicles (from Golgi) fuse with membrane and release contents (e.g., neurotransmitters). Increases membrane surface area, balancing endocytosis loss.

Cilia, Flagella & Microvilli

  • Motile cilia (9+2 microtubules): beat in unison; move mucus (respiratory tract) or ovum (uterine tube)
  • Primary cilium (9+0, nonmotile): almost every cell has one; sensory role (e.g., photoreceptors, kidney fluid detection)
  • Flagellum: only on sperm; 9+2 arrangement; propels the cell
  • Microvilli: tiny finger-like folds that greatly increase surface area for absorption (intestine, kidney tubules)
