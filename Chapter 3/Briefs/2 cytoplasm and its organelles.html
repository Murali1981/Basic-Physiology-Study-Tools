Cytoplasm & Its Organelles

Brief summary notes

Cytoplasm & Cytoskeleton

  • Cytoplasm = everything inside the cell except the nucleus (organelles + fluid cytosol)
  • Cytoskeleton: lattice of microfilaments & microtubules → structural support, organization of enzymes, and cell “musculature”
  • Enables amoeboid movement, forms spindle apparatus in cell division, and builds cilia/flagella
  • Acts as a “railway”: myosin moves cargo along actin; kinesin & dynein move cargo along microtubules
  • Inclusions: stored chemicals (glycogen granules, melanin, triglycerides)

Lysosomes

  • Membrane-bound “digestive system” of the cell; contain >60 enzymes in an acidic environment
  • Primary lysosome → fuses with food vacuole or organelle → secondary lysosome
  • Digest phagocytosed material and worn-out organelles (autophagy)
  • Residual bodies hold undigested waste (may be expelled by exocytosis)
  • Called “suicide bags” — rupture releases enzymes and can trigger apoptosis

Peroxisomes

  • Membrane-enclosed organelles that carry out oxidative reactions
  • Especially active in liver: oxidize toxic molecules (e.g., alcohol → acetaldehyde)
  • Produce H₂O₂, which is rapidly broken down by catalase (2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂)

Mitochondria

  • Present in all cells except mature RBCs; produce most of the cell’s energy (ATP)
  • Double membrane: smooth outer + inner membrane with folds (cristae) projecting into the matrix
  • Can migrate, divide, and contain their own circular DNA (endosymbiotic origin from bacteria-like organisms)
  • Produce superoxide radicals; mitochondrial DNA mutations may contribute to aging

Ribosomes

  • “Protein factories” — synthesize proteins from mRNA instructions
  • Found free in cytoplasm or attached to rough ER
  • Two subunits (30S & 50S); ribosomal RNA acts as ribozymes (catalytic RNA)

Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)

  • Rough (granular) ER: studded with ribosomes; abundant in protein-secreting cells
  • Smooth (agranular) ER: no ribosomes; steroid hormone synthesis/inactivation, Ca²⁺ storage (muscle), drug detoxification (liver)
  • Chronic drug use (alcohol, phenobarbital) → hypertrophy of smooth ER → tolerance

Golgi Complex

  • Stack of flattened, hollow sacs (cisternae)
  • Receives vesicles from ER → chemically modifies products → packages them into new vesicles
  • Outgoing vesicles may become lysosomes, secretory vesicles (exocytosis), or serve other roles
