Protein Synthesis & Secretion

Brief summary notes

Overview of Translation

  • mRNA carries the genetic code from the nucleus to the cytoplasm
  • Attaches to ribosomes (4 rRNA + 82 proteins → 2 subunits) forming polyribosomes (polysomes)
  • Every 3 bases on mRNA = a codon that specifies one amino acid
  • Sequence of codons → sequence of amino acids in the polypeptide

Transfer RNA (tRNA)

  • Single-stranded RNA folded into a cloverleaf / inverted “L” shape
  • One end has the anticodon (3 bases complementary to an mRNA codon)
  • Other end carries a specific amino acid
  • Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase enzymes attach the correct amino acid to each tRNA (20 types, one per amino acid)

Formation of a Polypeptide

  • tRNA anticodons bind sequentially to mRNA codons on the ribosome
  • Amino acids are joined by peptide bonds; the growing chain remains attached to the latest tRNA
  • Chain folds into secondary (helix) and tertiary structure as it grows
  • Chaperone proteins help the new polypeptide fold correctly and prevent toxic aggregates

Secretory Pathway (ER & Golgi)

  • Proteins for use inside the cell → free polyribosomes
  • Proteins for secretion or membranes → ribosomes on rough ER
  • A hydrophobic leader sequence directs the growing chain into the ER cisterna; the sequence is then removed
  • Example: insulin is processed from preproinsulin → proinsulin → mature insulin inside the ER
  • Proteins move to the Golgi complex, where they are:
    • Further modified (e.g., glycosylation)
    • Sorted by function/destination
    • Packaged into vesicles (secretory, lysosomal, or membrane-bound)

Protein Degradation

  • Regulatory proteins must be rapidly degraded for tight control
  • Lysosomal proteases digest many cellular proteins
  • Critical regulatory proteins are tagged with ubiquitin and degraded by the ATP-dependent proteasome
  • Example: stepwise degradation of cyclins drives progression through the cell cycle

Clinical Note

  • Huntington’s disease: CAG repeats (40–250) in the huntingtin gene → excess glutamine → neural degeneration
  • Fragile X syndrome: ≥200 CGG repeats in FMR1 gene → common genetic cause of intellectual disability
